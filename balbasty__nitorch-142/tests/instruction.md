# Issue

**Title:** FIX(spatial): one-voxel shift in fov_max / mean_space

## Description

## Summary

`compute_fov` returns one-based voxel coordinates (corners at 1 and N), but `fov_max` shifted the output space by `mn` instead of `mn - 1` (the correct line was there, commented out). As a result, the mean space of a single image was shifted by one voxel with respect to that image. Round-off errors (or the approximate `affine_mean`) could also add a voxel to the bounding box, e.g. a 20×16×8 image with a non-identity orientation got a 21×17×9 mean space.

The fix is in `nitorch/spatial/_affine.py`:
- shift by `mn - 1`;
- floor/ceil with a 1e-2 voxel tolerance.

## Impact

In `nitorch register`, the velocity grid lives in this mean space, so it was offset by one voxel from the images. Gradients are pushed onto the velocity grid with a zero boundary since a recent fix, which had two consequences:
- in 3D, one border slice of gradient was lost along each axis;
- along singleton axes (2D images, coarse pyramid levels of thin slabs), the whole gradient was lost, so nonlinear registration did not move at all.

`mean_space` / `fov_max` are also used by `nitorch meanspace`, autoreg, qMRI (relax) preprocessing and affine registration. Their output grids shift by one voxel (and lose a spurious extra voxel) with this fix.

Full 2D registration also needs the finite-difference fix in the companion PR (`claude/bugfix/grid-solver-options`). With both:
- the label-based oracle registration of synthetic 2D pairs goes from Dice 0.53 (no motion) to 0.95;
- on 4-slice slabs it goes from 0.90 to 0.95.
